Raveo

Raveo is a warm grotesque: a variable sans in seven weights and three widths, upright and italic, free and open source. It began as a fork of Inter. Version 2.0 adds the width axis, the italics and sharper stroke endings.

Optical size

Every letter is drawn twice. The text drawing has taller small letters and more room around them, so it stays clear at 12 or 14 pixels. The display drawing is tighter and finer. Browsers and design apps choose by type size on their own.

        Four hours to Vienna, the slow way

        The 07:42 leaves Prague from platform three and is in Vienna by ten to twelve. You could fly it in fifty minutes; almost nobody on this train would. There is a dining car, a river that keeps turning up on the left, and four hours in which nothing is asked of you.

        Past Pardubice the fields open out and the stations get smaller: a signal box, a platform, two people waiting for a train that is not this one. Moravia goes by in vineyards and church towers. Somebody opens a thermos. The conductor checks the same ticket twice and apologises both times.

        By Břeclav the announcements have switched to German, the coffee costs 20 Kč more, and it is better. The border is a bridge you notice only because the phone says so. At 11:49 the train stops under the glass roof of Wien Hbf, on time to the minute, and everyone stands up at once.

            About

            Where it comes from.

            Raveo was originally built on Inter by Rasmus Andersson. It keeps Inter's proportions and its legibility on screens, with warmer letterforms, darker weights and a width axis of its own, from narrow to wide. It is a variable family meant for interfaces, running text and headlines.
